Same-day appointments

Dental emergencies

There is nothing quite like toothache. We understand that, and we aim to see all new patients the same day, within our working hours.

Call us as early in the day as you can

Emergency slots are held open each morning and fill quickly. The earlier you ring, the more likely we can see you today.

Getting you comfortable, quickly

We have a great deal of practice at working out what is actually causing the pain — and we endeavour to render you pain‑free as soon as possible.

Sometimes that means definitive treatment there and then. Sometimes it means settling things down first — a dressing, a temporary filling, drainage — and booking the proper repair once you are out of pain and can think straight. Either way you will leave knowing what happened and what comes next.

A good number of our most nervous patients first came to us in an emergency, expecting the worst, and stayed as regular patients afterwards. If that sounds like you, say so when you ring.

Call us straight away if you have

  • Toothache that keeps you awake or is not settling with painkillers
  • Facial swelling, or swelling around a tooth or gum
  • A tooth knocked out, loosened or broken
  • A lost filling, crown or bridge
  • Bleeding that will not stop after an extraction
  • Pain when biting, or sensitivity that lingers for minutes
  • Something sharp cutting your tongue or cheek
Swelling that affects your eye, your breathing or your ability to swallow needs urgent medical attention. Go to A&E or call 999 — do not wait for a dental appointment.

Before you get here

What you can do in the meantime

A knocked-out adult tooth

Hold it by the crown, not the root. If it is dirty, rinse it briefly in milk. Put it back in the socket if you can and bite gently on a clean cloth; if not, keep it in milk and ring us immediately. Time matters enormously here.

Toothache

Take your usual painkiller at the dose on the packet — ibuprofen and paracetamol, if they suit you, work well together. Never hold an aspirin against the gum: it burns the tissue. A cold compress on the cheek can help.

A lost filling or crown

Keep the crown safe and bring it with you. Avoid chewing on that side, and keep the area clean. A pharmacy temporary filling kit is fine as a stopgap for a day or two, but it is not a repair.

Outside our opening hours, NHS urgent dental care in Portsmouth can be reached through NHS 111, which will direct you to the right service. For anything life‑threatening, call 999.
